WinoGrande Phase 6 Brief

Follow-up to the Phase 5 brief.

You asked for the following, and this brief answers each in turn:

  • one principled dial, turned on the same WinoGrande items, with the expected result written down before the experiment;
  • different tasks graded in advance;
  • dense counterparts and bigger models in the same family;
  • circuits down to experts and neurons.

We fixed 45 predictions in a commit before any GPU run.

Answers at a glance

What moves the attention / FFN split on the same items?Mainly the answer type. When the answer is an entity, attention does most of the repair; when it is an attribute, the FFN / experts do. Stating the attribute next to its entity moves the attribute answers toward attention in 7 of 8 models. Qwen3-30B-A3B ignores the statement.
Dense vs MoE?No difference at matched architecture. Mistral-7B and Mixtral-8x7B agree within ±0.10 on all 10 tasks; Qwen2-7B and Qwen2-57B agree on 6 of 7.
More capacity?The dense Qwen3 series is flat from 4B to 14B. Qwen3-30B-A3B departs in both directions, but that is not a size trend. Mixtral-8x22B waits for your go.
Graded tasks, predicted in advance?The top of the predicted order held: IOI, induction and explicit binding look-up are the most attention-heavy. The bottom failed: antonyms sit near 0.5, not near 0.2.
Associativity as a dial?Weak: the spread is 0.10. When a statement contradicts the prior, the experts write the prior answer and attention overrides it.
Experts down to neurons?A handful of neurons carry 80 % of an expert's effect (2–4 of 768 in Qwen3, 4–17 of 14,336 in Mixtral), but different neurons for different prompts. Mixtral's experts are Mistral-7B's MLP, neuron for neuron.
The ICML paper (neuron circuits, RelP) on MoE?It ports and works. At the final token it adds little to what we have; its value is at earlier positions. For expert + head circuits it is not needed.
Concept circuits of experts + heads?In both models, the concept circuits hurt their own tasks much more than other tasks. Experts are more concept-specific than heads in 3 of 6 cases; in the country circuits and in Mixtral's person-role circuit, the heads are more specific than the experts.
Is the same-instance dial new?As far as our literature scan found, yes. Context-vs-memory contrasts exist for dense models, but not a 3+ level dial on the same items with an attention / FFN split, and nothing in MoE.

The dial: one WinoGrande twin, different evidence

Only the evidence changes. The question, the swapped word and the format stay fixed.

E1 stated The jug was empty and the pot was full. Fill in the blank: The _ was empty. Answer: the → " jug" E1p paraphrase The jug was drained and the pot was filled. … → " jug" E2 negation The jug was not full and the pot was not empty. … → " jug" E3 event The restaurant worker poured water from the jug into the pot. … → " jug" entity → attribute, same levels … , so the jug was → " empty"
  • Filters as in Phase 5: single-token answers; one swapped token; correct both ways by ≥ 1 logit; both orders of the two statements must pass.
  • Pilots before any patching: our first format (…, so the _ was empty) gave chance-level answers in both models. A one-line layout change fixed it, and the format was chosen by a rule written before the pilot.
  • Small cells: pooled (no held-out split) or behaviour only, by a fixed rule.

2 · The answer type sets the split; stating the binding moves it

blue
Attribute → entity: the answer is an entity, e.g. " jug".
orange
Entity → attribute: the answer is an attribute, e.g. " empty".
● → ◆
From the binding implied by the event (E3) to the binding stated outright (E1). Open markers are pooled cells.
Takeaway.
  • Entity answers are attention-heavy at every level (0.48–0.83). This holds even with no evidence at all (Qwen3: 0.73).
  • Attribute answers are FFN-heavy when the attribute must be inferred (0.18–0.34).
  • Stating the attribute moves attribute answers toward attention in 7 of 8 models.
    • Qwen3-4B 0.31 → 0.58, Qwen3-8B 0.28 → 0.55, Qwen3-14B 0.21 → 0.46.
    • Qwen2 twins 0.21 → 0.37–0.38, Mistral 0.31 → 0.49, Mixtral 0.34 → 0.50.
    • The direction gap roughly halves.
  • Qwen3-30B-A3B is the exception (0.18 → 0.22). Its gap stays at +0.59. Our best guess before the run was that the gap closes; for this model that guess failed.
  • A paraphrase works as well as the exact words (E1p = E1 within 0.07). The binding matters, not copying the word. This also explains Phase 5's small copy effect: an "Options:" list makes the word available without binding it to an entity.
  • Negation fails in every model. In the entity question the models choose the entity that sits next to the matching word. In the Qwen3 family that wrong answer wins 41–60 % of pairs. This level is reported as behaviour only.

3 · Dense parent vs MoE child, and capacity

  • Mistral-7B → Mixtral-8x7B, identical prompts: every task within ±0.10 (MoE − dense −0.03 to +0.06). The order of the tasks is preserved (Spearman 0.98).
  • Qwen2-7B → Qwen2-57B-A14B: 6 of 7 tasks within ±0.10; antonyms −0.11 (Spearman 0.89).
  • The always-on shared expert carries most of Qwen2-57B's expert repair: 0.67–0.77 on every task (entity answers, antonyms, object property, CounterFact, IOI) except the WinoGrande attribute answers, where it carries about half (0.43 stated, 0.51 inferred).
  • Mixtral is an upcycled Mistral. Every layer-20 expert matches Mistral-7B's layer-20 MLP neuron by neuron. The WinoGrande expert's top-10 neurons are 8 of Mistral's own top neurons.
Takeaway.
  • At matched architecture the split is a property of the task, not of dense vs sparse FFNs. We had written down three hypotheses: invariant (our best guess), "more FFN capacity takes work from attention", and "sparsity pushes work to attention". Only "invariant" held.
  • The dense Qwen3 series is flat from 4B to 14B. Qwen3-30B-A3B is more attention-heavy on copy tasks (IOI 0.92 vs 0.73) and less on attribute answers. With no Qwen3 dense twin, that is a property of this model, not a capacity trend.
  • Qwen2-57B must run in fp32. Its expert routers are nearly uniform, so bf16 rounding changes most routing decisions, even in the reference implementation. In bf16 the attention share would read 0.68 instead of 0.58.

  • The ICML method (RelP on neurons), ported to MoE with frozen routing, piloted on OLMoE and Mistral-7B. At the last token it ranks neurons about as well as our forward scores.
    • On factual recall, three quarters of the neuron effect sits at the subject and relation positions. A last-token-only circuit gets 0.23–0.50 of the drop.
    • That is where the method helps. It is not needed for expert + head circuits.
  • Concept circuits. Each circuit is the set of heads and experts shared by two or more tasks of one concept. Knocking it out removes, from its own tasks vs other tasks:

  • Held:

8 · Checks and lessons

  • Metric: the ranking of tasks is the same under logit difference, normalised logit difference and probability (Spearman ≥ 0.97).
  • Causal handle: on the stated entity question, knocking out the top-3 heads or experts lowers the attention or expert repair by 21–31 % more than matched random ones (12–19 % on attribute answers). The share itself moves only 0.02–0.07, so the split is spread over many components.
  • Corruption site: swapping the evidence instead of the question changes the share by only 0.03–0.05.
  • Format matters more than expected: one format change took a base model from chance to 99 % on the same facts. Every new format now gets a behaviour pilot with a fixed decision rule first.
  • Cutting sentences is risky: one builder left "… because the one on." Every prompt is now checked by reassembling the original sentence.
  • Check precision per model: the bf16 routing of a flat-router MoE can be noise.
